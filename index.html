The big question

Smaller than last year — but last year was a record

2026 is still the second biggest cashew crop ever grown. The drop is real, but small. And as the season went on, the estimates kept going up, not down.

World raw cashew crop, 2026

6.0million tonnes
Last year was 6.2–6.4 million tonnes — the biggest ever
▼ 3–6% vs last year

But we got less kernel out of it

−6 to −10%
A bag of West African nuts gave 2.0–2.5 lbs less kernel than last year. So the edible cashew we actually got fell about twice as fast as the crop did.

Four groups measured the same crop — million tonnes

their 2026 number their own 2025 number

They don't even agree on the direction — Rotterdam and VINACAS say the crop went up 3%. So give a range, never one number. And note that FAOSTAT counts a different way again, around 60% lower. Never put the two in the same table.

Careful: don't just add these up

Benin's export ban leaked 90,000–100,000 tonnes into Nigeria and Togo. Nigeria's 400,000 figure already includes 70,000–80,000 tonnes of Benin's nuts. Ghana's official number counts nuts just passing through. Add them up carelessly and you overcount West Africa by 150,000–250,000 tonnes.

Asia · India · Vietnam · Cambodia

Asia grew more — and one popular story is simply wrong

India

770,000–810,000tonnes
≈ about the same
Nobody has published an official 2026 number. Last year's official figure was 802,349 tonnes. The Konkan belt — a third of India's crop — is down 11%: Maharashtra −5%, Karnataka −20%, Goa −28%. Careful: the 825,000 figure going around is an old record, not this year's crop.

Vietnam

323,000tonnes
▲ 4.6%
You may have heard Vietnam's crop collapsed 30–50%. It didn't. The country grew more. Those alarming numbers came from single farms and villages, not the whole country. And Vietnam's own crop is only about a tenth of what its factories shell anyway.

Cambodia

0.9–1.0million tonnes
▲ exports up 10–28%
Cambodia now decides how much raw nut this region has — and has the least reliable numbers of anyone. Its first-half exports alone are bigger than most estimates of its whole-year crop. The growth comes from new trees, not better yields. Over 90% leaves unshelled, almost all of it to Vietnam.

Where Vietnam bought its 1.81 million tonnes of raw nuts — first half of 2026

Cambodia alone sends Vietnam more raw nuts than Vietnam grows five times over. That means bad weather in Cambodia now hits Vietnamese factories directly — a risk that simply didn't exist a few years ago.

Also in Asia

  • Indonesia, about 150,000 tonnes, same as last year. Its nuts give the best kernel yield in Asia, but the crop is too small to change the world picture. It was also the only origin where farmers were paid less this year, down 8.6%.
  • Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, the Philippines — all small, and none of them published reliable 2026 crop figures.

Resourced fromVietnam Ministry of Agriculture & Environment (first half 2026: 323,400 tonnes, up 4.6%) · VINACAS · Cambodian customs and the Cashew Nut Association of Cambodia · Xinhua · India's DCCD · Ministry of Agriculture state estimates via Navhind Times · APEDA

Volumes were fine. Quality and government rules were not.

How much they grow vs how much they sell

India and Vietnam are in completely different businesses

India grows 2.5× more raw cashew than Vietnam — and sells abroad about one twenty-second as much. That isn't failure. It's a different business.

55×
Vietnam sells 2.5 tonnes of kernel for every tonne it grows. India sells 0.045.
9%
of India's cashew is sold abroad. Over 350,000 tonnes is eaten at home — the world's biggest market, growing ~7% a year.
27%
India's kernel costs more — USD 3.95–4.10 a pound against Vietnam's 3.05–3.30 — and the gap is growing
1.7×
Vietnam shells 1.7× more nuts than India while growing 40% as many. Both buy from abroad — the crop size isn't the problem.

Quality · how much kernel comes out of a bag

This year, quality mattered more than quantity

Every 80 kg bag of raw nuts gives you a certain weight of edible kernel. Higher is better. Below about 45 lbs, a factory starts losing money on that bag.

The most useful lesson this year

Nigerian nuts arrived at the farm gate at 42–45 lbs and left the warehouse at 45–50 lbs — just from drying them properly. How you handle the nut matters as much as the tree. Ghana showed the opposite, arriving far too wet.

Resourced fromAfrican Cashew Alliance AfriCashewSplits 2026 bulletins · the 2.0–2.5 lb decline reported by OFI via the Cardassilaris Cashew Market Report, May 2026. Worth remembering: last year was an unusually good year for quality, so this year is a return to normal rather than a crisis.

Prices · raw nuts and kernel

Raw nut prices stayed high. Kernel prices didn't.

The African Cashew Alliance put it plainly in June: "Costs have risen and [raw cashew] stocks are in strong hands… [raw cashew] prices may need to come into line a little." In other words, the factories are absorbing the whole gap.

What a pound of kernel sells for — now vs a year ago, USD

India has printed USD 3.98 four times in the same tables — a year ago, a month ago, two weeks ago and now. Frozen, while Vietnam came down. African kernel earns a premium for quality and reliability.

The hidden cost

Broken pieces sell for only 45–65% of whole kernel. Poorer nuts break more, so what you actually earn ends up well below the headline price.

Looking ahead · what to watch in 2027

Three risks for next year — only one is about weather

01 · El Niño — but this time on Cambodia

The Alliance expects the effect "most likely to be felt in Cambodia and Vietnam". The line that matters — "during past events Cambodia was not as important a producer as it is now." At a million tonnes and over half of Vietnam's raw material, bad weather there now hits Vietnamese factories directly.

To be fair: the last two El Niño events barely touched cashew, and one respected analyst publicly called the alarmist West African claims "incorrect and irresponsible". A real risk, but arguable in size.

02 · Factories closing

The risk the crop numbers hide completely. Less kernel per bag, firm raw nut prices and softer kernel prices broke factory economics everywhere at once.

  • Palasa — about 300 units stopped in April 2026
  • Andhra's ~700 units running at half capacity
  • Kerala down to ~100 working factories, from 830
  • Workers 300,000 → about 100,000

Next year may start with fewer factories than this year ended with — which pushes kernel prices up no matter how big the crop is.

03 · Governments changing the rules

This year alone: Benin's ban leaked 90,000–100,000 tonnes · Burkina Faso stopped exports and stranded 180,000 tonnes · Senegal added a USD 65 a tonne tax · Côte d'Ivoire gave its own factories a 10-day head start.

None of this changed how much cashew exists. All of it changed who could buy it. India's buying list was rewritten in a single season:

  • Togo −84% · Côte d'Ivoire −55%
  • Tanzania became #1 · Mozambique doubled

What to do about it

Think about supply route by route, not country by country — and buy on quality, not tonnage.

Early-season crop estimates in cashew are almost always too low, and they get revised upward. Anyone who believed the February panic paid for it later. Expect the same pattern early next year — and expect contracts to start pricing kernel yield directly after a season like this one.
